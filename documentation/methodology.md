# Methodology

## Data preparation

The group combined employee, subscription, ChatGPT, and Copilot usage records using employee identifiers. Cleaning included lookup reconciliation, activity-status filtering, missing-value handling, category standardization, and validation of merged fields.

## Metrics

- Adoption: number of employees with observed tool usage
- Subscription coverage: share of employees with access to a paid tool
- Usage intensity: prompts or messages adjusted by active usage days
- Cross-tool adoption: employees using both ChatGPT and Copilot
- Dormant subscription: subscribed account with no observed usage
- Frequency tier: super-active, high-frequency, low-frequency, or inactive Copilot user

## Segmentation

Results were compared across:

- Producer and other job categories
- Full-time, part-time, and contingent workers
- Managers and non-managers
- Organizational work groups

## Interpretation boundary

The results describe observed usage and subscription patterns. They do not establish that AI tool access caused differences in productivity or work quality.
